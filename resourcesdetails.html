Trade Facilitation Reform in Bangladesh: A Path to Single Window Implementation

Published 14 May 2026 • 12-minute read • 12 pages • Dr. Nusrat Jahan · GIET Foundation

1. The Problem: Complexity as a Hidden Tax on Trade

Bangladesh's import and export procedures involve an average of 14 distinct document requirements, interactions with up to 9 separate government agencies, and physical inspection rates that remain among the highest in South Asia. At Benapole, Bangladesh's busiest land port, average import clearance times stand at 72 hours — compared to a regional benchmark of 18–24 hours for ports of comparable volume in India, Thailand, and Vietnam.

2. The Single Window Concept: What It Is and What It Is Not

A National Single Window (NSW) is a coordinated border management system that allows traders to submit all trade-related documentation through a single electronic portal, with the information shared automatically across all relevant government agencies. The World Trade Organization's Trade Facilitation Agreement (TFA), which Bangladesh ratified in 2016, commits member states to endeavour to establish or maintain a Single Window system.

What Makes a Single Window Succeed

  • Legal mandate for inter-agency data sharing with no opt-out provisions for designated agencies.
  • A dedicated Programme Management Office with cross-agency representation and Cabinet-level sponsorship.
  • Risk-based selectivity replacing blanket physical inspection — typically requiring intelligence system investment.
  • Phased rollout starting with high-volume, lower-complexity transactions and agencies.
  • Parallel trader onboarding programmes targeting SME exporters who lack digital capacity.

3. Why Previous Attempts Have Stalled

Bangladesh has launched two prior Single Window initiatives — in 2011 and 2018 — both of which stalled at the pilot stage. GIET's diagnostic work identifies four structural failure modes that recurred in both attempts.

4. A Phased Implementation Roadmap

GIET recommends a three-phase implementation strategy spanning five years, designed to deliver early wins while building the institutional architecture required for full-scale deployment.

Phase 1 (Years 1–2): Foundation and Pilot

Establish the legal basis for the NSW through amendment of the Customs Act and the issuance of inter-agency data sharing directives. Create the NSW Programme Management Office within NBR with seconded staff from five priority agencies. Launch a limited pilot covering air freight at Shahjalal International Airport — the highest-value, lowest-volume entry point — with five agencies and 20 large registered importers.

GIET's policy research combines quantitative diagnostic work with structured stakeholder consultation to produce implementable recommendations.
